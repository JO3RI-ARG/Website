Heating an off-grid house

Coming from Belgium where our heating was with city gas, we had to do it differently.

How to heat the bedrooms

November 21, 2026

When my wife and I were planning on how to design our house, one thing was clear from the start, we sleep downstairs and living room, kitchen and bathroom are upstairs. The house is on a slope, so both levels have a door going outside. Why sleep downstairs? Well, it's hot in summers, so cooler downstairs, heat rises you know.

But in winters (yes we have winters, and sometimes a little bit of snow), downstairs is cold, so how do we solve that, without adding more rocket stoves and chimneys? I started looking into central heating and using the rocket stove as the heat source. It took me a long time to find a spiral and finally I found one on Amazon. This is the link (it might not stay available, but I'll post it as a reference) https://www.amazon.es/-/en/gp/product/B0GWQKRTV6

For me the most important thing was that the spiral would not be inside the chimney pipe, so the chimney would be easy to clean. I don't know yet how well it will perform, so that's up for a next blog post. The rocket stove performs well, and we know, because we already used it in the winter of 2025-2026.

HOT HOT HOT

October 28, 2026

Back in Belgium heating was done with a central heating system connected to city gas. Cold? push a button and we had a warm house, ... and a monthly bill and gas came from ... well well Russia. Then there was war and prices went up and Belgium would never buy gas from Russia again and then it just did as before. Anyway ... No city gas in Portugal and we're off-grid, so ... we needed something else.

What you see in the picture is a rocket stove. We chose this over a conventional fireplace because it wastes far less of the wood's energy. A fireplace sends most of its heat straight up the chimney, while a rocket stove burns small pieces of wood very hot inside an insulated vertical riser, so the smoke burns off before it leaves. The remaining heat is pulled out of the exhaust, which means less wood, less smoke and a warmer house.

Burning wood is bad, you say? Well, that depends, we made the choice because we happen to have wood on our land. Well we had, because most burned down in the 2025 fires, but it's already regrowing. AND that makes us more independent.
